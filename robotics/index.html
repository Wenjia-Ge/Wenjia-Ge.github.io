Research interests

I want robots to perceive and move through real environments more reliably: navigating accurately, avoiding obstacles, and recognizing the state of the people around them.

My work so far sits on the data and systems side of that problem. At the AI4CE Lab I build real-world 3D datasets for embodied AI and world models. In medical robotics I built a human-in-the-loop pipeline from a haptic device into a surgical simulation. In industry I tested robots across the full perception, decision, and action loop. I am also exploring robot learning, vision-language-action models, and humanoid robotics.

Most relevant work

Visual spatial data for robot perception and training

NYU AI4CE Lab, advised by Prof. Chen Feng, 2026 to present

I collect visual and spatial data with MetaCam across indoor and outdoor scenes and process the raw output into point clouds and 3D scene representations. I inspect reconstructions in CloudCompare and MeshLab, flag artifacts, and build preprocessing and visualization workflows that raise dataset quality for robot training and world models. I also build HPC workflows for ingestion, 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction, and quality control of large-scale navigation data.

I am a student leader on the lab's Wanderland project, a vision-language navigation benchmark built on real-world 3D reconstructions and a CVPR 2026 Highlight. I collect data, track the team's collection progress, and onboard new members. Wanderland project page. Raw data is not public because of lab policy.

Medical robotics: surgical navigation and tactile sensing

Research Assistant, advised by Prof. Rui Li, Feb to Aug 2026

A simulation workflow for vascular modeling, path planning, and robot visualization in minimally invasive surgery research. It models the robot's curvature and motion inside constrained anatomy. I processed CTA-based anatomical models, extracted vessel centerlines with VMTK, and built the pipeline from a 3D Systems Touch haptic device through ROS2 into RViz and 3D Slicer. I handled hardware integration, real-time visualization, and validation, and resolved instability from WSL2 and USB passthrough.

On the sensing side, I set up and validated two tactile sensors with an Arduino and Python (pyserial) workflow that streams, plots, and analyzes both channels in real time. I characterized baseline noise and contact response, tuned detection thresholds from measured ADC ranges, and measured cross-talk between channels during simultaneous operation. I also explored waterproof encapsulation with PDMS, Ecoflex, and Parylene C, and defined bare-versus-coated metrics for sensitivity, baseline stability, noise, and response time.

Testing robots across the perception, decision, and action loop

Research Intern, Embodied AI and Robotics, AI² Robotics, Shenzhen, Oct 2025 to Jan 2026

I validated robot modules on their own, then evaluated them in the full loop across seven subsystems: perception, localization and mapping, task planning, motion control, manipulation, communications, and safety. For each one I designed test protocols and metrics, such as recognition accuracy under changing light and occlusion, localization drift and recovery, and stopping distance on emergency stop.

I injected message delay, packet loss, sensor disconnection, and process restarts to measure fault detection and recovery, and documented failure cases with the algorithm, hardware, and systems teams.

  • System validation
  • Fault injection
  • Test protocols
  • Embodied AI

IMU-based gesture recognition on STM32

Individual embedded systems project, STM32L475 Discovery board

A motion-driven gesture recognition system built around IMU sensing, gesture encoding, and onboard feedback. I implemented gyroscope calibration, three-step gesture recording, and dominant-axis matching, using the LSM6DSL IMU, UART monitoring, and LED feedback for interaction and debugging. The whole flow is written in C on STM32Cube and PlatformIO.
