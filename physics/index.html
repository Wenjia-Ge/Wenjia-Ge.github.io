Research interests

Physics is where I started, and it is the field I most want to return to. I am interested in how thin films and interfaces shape device performance, how defects and recombination limit it, and how to detect weak signals while keeping noise low and measurements stable.

I am open across semiconductor, optoelectronic, and optical materials, optical measurement and sensing, and quantum devices, including 2D and superconducting thin films. My undergraduate thesis gave me hands-on fabrication and characterization, my ICONE30 paper gave me careful numerical verification, and my engineering work since then has kept me close to sensors and measurement.

Most relevant work

Sb2S3 thin-film solar cells

Undergraduate thesis, Shenzhen University, Outstanding Thesis Award

I fabricated CdS buffer layers and Sb2S3 absorber films by chemical bath deposition and built planar FTO/CdS/Sb2S3/Spiro-OMeTAD/Au solar cells from them. The question was whether a thioacetamide surface treatment could improve the absorber surface and its interface, so I compared treated devices against untreated controls.

I characterized crystallinity, morphology, and wettability with XRD, SEM, and contact-angle measurements, and device behavior with J-V and EQE. I operated the monochromator and spectrometer setup for EQE myself. From EQE, the Urbach energy dropped from 27 to 22 meV. From dark J-V, the ideality factor dropped from 3.51 to 2.44. Both point to less defect-related recombination, and power conversion efficiency rose from 4.43% to 5.43%.

Verifying the Method of Characteristics

First-author paper, ICONE30, Kyoto, Japan, 2023

I analyzed flat-source and linear-source approximations of the Method of Characteristics, a numerical method used in reactor physics, and verified them with the Method of Manufactured Solutions. I implemented the verification and measured error convergence across meshes and rays. In planar geometry, the flat-source approximation is second-order accurate and the linear-source approximation reaches fourth-order accuracy.

Wenjia Ge, et al. Application of the Method of Manufactured Solutions to Verify a Nuclear Reactor Physics Computational Code using the Method of Characteristics. ICONE30, Kyoto, Japan, 2023.

Tactile sensor characterization for medical robotics

Research Assistant, advised by Prof. Rui Li, Feb to Aug 2026

On the sensing side, I set up and validated two tactile sensors with an Arduino and Python (pyserial) workflow that streams, plots, and analyzes both channels in real time. I characterized baseline noise and contact response, tuned detection thresholds from measured ADC ranges, and measured cross-talk between channels during simultaneous operation. I also explored waterproof encapsulation with PDMS, Ecoflex, and Parylene C, and defined bare-versus-coated metrics for sensitivity, baseline stability, noise, and response time.

The same project included a surgical navigation simulation: a concentric-tube robot moving through vascular anatomy, with a 3D Systems Touch haptic device connected through ROS2 to RViz, 3D Slicer, and VMTK centerline models.

Visual spatial data for robot perception and training

NYU AI4CE Lab, advised by Prof. Chen Feng, 2026 to present

I capture real indoor and outdoor scenes with MetaCam, which combines LiDAR and fisheye cameras, and process the raw output into point clouds and 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions. Much of the work is quality control: inspecting reconstructions in CloudCompare and MeshLab and checking for gaps and artifacts. I build the HPC workflows for preprocessing and reconstruction.

I am a student leader on the lab's Wanderland project, a vision-language navigation benchmark built on real-world 3D reconstructions and a CVPR 2026 Highlight. I collect data, track the team's collection progress, and onboard new members. Wanderland project page. Raw data is not public because of lab policy.
